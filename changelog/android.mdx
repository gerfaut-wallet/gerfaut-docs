## 0.1.0

The first release: Gerfaut for Android.

### Added

- Watch a wallet from what you already have: a descriptor, an extended or single public key, a plain address, a multisig setup, a BSMS record or another wallet's export file. Paste it, pick a file, or scan the QR code, static or animated. Gerfaut works out the format on its own and shows what it understood before it stores anything.
- Balance, transaction history and UTXOs per wallet, with the fiat value of the day and one action to hide every amount on screen.
- Hold a wallet card and drag it into the order you want, on the home screen and in the settings. When the vault cannot keep the new order, a note above the cards says so, with a button to try again.
- Receive addresses with their QR code, the used ones marked, and a warning past the gap limit.
- A Policy page for every wallet: what the descriptor allows, branch by branch, with each timelock counted down in blocks and in days.
- Broadcast a signed transaction or a PSBT, read back in plain language before it goes anywhere. When no backend could confirm a coin it spends, Gerfaut marks the amounts and the fee as the file's claims rather than the chain's. It then says what to check before you send.
- Your own node over Electrum or Esplora, or one of the public servers. Gerfaut shows a self-signed certificate and trusts it once, then stops on any other certificate from that host until you decide.
- A Tor client built in: a `.onion` backend works with no Orbot installed.
- An encrypted backup file, and the same backup as an animated QR code, so two devices sync with no server in between. The export page asks for a passphrase of several words, since someone who copies the file can try passwords offline. The restore page lists what the file holds, wallet by wallet and node setting by node setting, before it writes anything. If the system's save dialog cannot open, the page says so in place, like any other failure.
- The vault stays out of Android's own backups and device-to-device transfers. It leaves the phone only as a Gerfaut backup, sealed under your password.
- Gerfaut never overwrites a vault this phone can no longer open. It sets the vault aside under another name and offers to start over, with the restore page first.
- A PIN or password lock with biometrics, attempts slowed after three tries and still slowed after a restart, and a calculator disguise for the icon.
- Gerfaut marks a descriptor or a policy you copy from it as sensitive on the clipboard. Android shows no preview of it and keeps it out of its history. Gerfaut also clears it from the clipboard a minute later, unless you copied something else since. The confirmation says `for 1 minute`.
- Home screen widgets for the price, the balance (hidden by default) and the block height.
- Local notifications when a sync finds something new. Nothing leaves the phone for them.
- Live, a choice under `Check for transactions` in **Settings** > **Notifications**. Gerfaut keeps a connection open to the backend your syncs already use and tells you when a transaction reaches the mempool, then again at its first confirmation. Over an Electrum server, the notice comes within seconds. Gerfaut can only poll an Esplora server, which takes longer. With the `Automatic` backend, Live connects first to an Electrum server run by one of the operators your syncs rotate through. Live runs with Gerfaut closed and comes back after the phone restarts. Android requires a small permanent notification for it, which has a `Stop` button. A sheet explains all this before Live starts, then asks for the battery exemption, which you can refuse. The server learns what a sync already tells it, plus how long your phone stays connected and from where. A check every 15 minutes stays scheduled for whenever Android stops Live. Force-stopping Gerfaut ends Live until you open Gerfaut again, which then announces what arrived meanwhile. Turning on the calculator disguise turns Live off and clears Gerfaut's notifications from the shade.
- On a Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO, Huawei, Honor, Samsung, OnePlus, Oppo or realme phone, the Live sheet lists the switches to turn on in the maker's own battery manager. It also opens Gerfaut's app info page, where most of them live. No app can flip those switches for you. Once you granted Android's battery exemption, the sheet leaves out a step that only repeats it.
- A `This is my node` switch under the address of your own Electrum or Esplora server, in **Settings** > **Network**. With it on, Live follows up to 20,000 addresses instead of 2,000. Leave it off for a server you do not run, which would refuse most of them and learn every one. Saving the server address again keeps the switch as it was.
- When Live cannot follow every address, **Settings** > **Notifications** says how many addresses of how many wallets wait for the next sync, and how to lift the limit. Each wallet then shows `Live`, `Partly live` or `At next sync` on its card, on its page and in **Settings** > **Wallets**. On your own node, when the server itself refuses addresses, the note names the server setting to raise.
- `Always watch live first`, under **Settings** > **Wallets** > **Advanced**, picks the wallets Live follows before the others when it cannot follow every address.
- When Android stops letting Gerfaut's notifications through, from the system settings or by blocking the channel, **Settings** > **Notifications** says so in amber under `New transactions`. A button leads to the system settings. Live stops meanwhile, since it would have nothing to say, and starts again once the notifications get through.
- While Gerfaut is disguised, it greys out the notification settings, with a line that says a notification would show the name Gerfaut. The sheet that turns the disguise on says so before you confirm.
- Gerfaut announces a transaction once when it appears and once when it confirms, whichever part of Gerfaut saw it first. A fee bump brings no second notice, and its confirmation takes the place of the pending one. When an incoming payment leaves the mempool before it confirms, and nothing else pays the wallet in its place, one more notice says it is no longer coming. While an app lock is set, a notification names no wallet and no amount. Titled `Gerfaut`, it only says what happened, as in `New transaction · pending`. Without a lock, the title is the wallet name, and hiding the balances keeps the amount out of it.
- Signet, testnet4 and regtest alongside mainnet.
- One APK per processor architecture instead of one carrying all three: 50 MB to download on a 64-bit ARM phone rather than 127.
- Reproducible builds. The APKs come out of a container that pins every tool version, and the same commit always gives the same bytes. Rebuild them yourself and compare: only the signature differs. See [Verify a download](/getting-started/verify-your-download#rebuild-it-yourself).
- A notice on the home screen when a newer release is out, with a button to the release page and a `Later` button that closes it for that release. Gerfaut asks GitHub for the latest release once a day at most, when you open it. When one of your nodes is a `.onion` address, the request goes through Tor, or not at all when Tor is out of reach. Gerfaut asks nothing while it is disguised. **Settings** > **About** turns the automatic check off and keeps the check on demand.

### Changed

- Syncs use far less data. Gerfaut downloads only what a wallet does not have yet. A payment that Live notices costs a few kilobytes instead of the wallet's whole history. Gerfaut still reads a wallet in full when the vault opens and once a day.
- Fiat values group their thousands with a space, like amounts in BTC and sats.
- In Add a wallet, the script type choices give the address prefixes of the wallet's network, such as `tb1q` on signet and testnet4.
- Broadcast warns in red when a signature leaves the outputs open (`SIGHASH_NONE` or `SIGHASH_SINGLE`). Whoever relays such a transaction can send that money elsewhere. The page tells you not to send it as it is. When the outputs pay more than the inputs bring, the last check before sending says the network would refuse it.
- Coming back from a file picker, a save dialog or a share sheet more than ten minutes after it opened asks for the app lock again. That trip used to leave Gerfaut unlocked, however long it lasted.
- A release build no longer writes the text of an unexpected error to the system log, where it could quote an address.
- When every wallet is on the selected network, the backup export reads `All wallets (N)` instead of offering a choice of one.
- In Add a wallet, an input that fits a single network, such as a mainnet address, names that network instead of offering a choice of one.
- The Policy page reads like the desktop one. Primary paths come first, then recovery and emergency ones, each from the nearest lock to the farthest. One coin counts like several, as in `1 of 1 coin unlocked`. A `Next coin` line gives the wait of the first coin to unlock, in blocks and in days.
- On the Policy page, a coin that Gerfaut cannot count down before the first sync reads `next known after the first sync` instead of `next in an unknown time`.
- TalkBack calls the pin icon `Pin`, like the desktop app, instead of `Map pin`.

### Fixed

- The vault opens in one place at a time. When Gerfaut, a background check and Live start together, they share that one opening instead of the later ones failing. If another part of Gerfaut still holds the vault, Gerfaut says it is already open and offers to try again. It never sets that vault aside. A background check quietly skips its turn.
- Receive offers only addresses nobody has paid yet, and stops 200 addresses past the next unused one. The gap limit warning counts unused addresses in a row, as scanning software does. A descriptor with a single address offers no next one.
- The wallet file picker shows every file, so you can pick a `.bsms` or `.desc` file. It used to grey them out.
- When Gerfaut refuses a backend, for example a host with a port still in it, the reason shows under the `Save backend` button. The save used to stop without a word.
- Gerfaut refuses a private key in a pasted wallet, a file or a backup with a sentence that says what to bring instead. A descriptor the wallet engine refuses says so, with the reason the engine gives.
- The `UTXOs` tab keeps its rows on screen while a sync reads them again, instead of flashing `Loading UTXOs…`. A coin with no address says `n/a`. TalkBack says which chip holds the outpoint and which holds the address.
- Gerfaut refuses a QR code that announces more than 10,000 parts before it reads it. One such frame used to make Gerfaut quit.
- Importing a wallet, a transaction or a backup from a file that is too large, such as a video, says so. Gerfaut used to read the whole file first, which could make it quit. A wallet file that is not text says so too.
- Turning Live off and on again within a few seconds starts it again. It used to stay off until Gerfaut came back to the screen.
- With Live on, removing your last wallet left the permanent notification on `Connecting…` for good. Live now stops, notification included, while the selected network has no wallet. It starts again when you add one.
- With the screen off, Live keeps the phone awake while it checks the server and until it has announced a new transaction. The notification then arrives within seconds rather than at the next check. A status that changes nothing keeps nothing awake.
- When Android refuses to restart Live, Gerfaut waits longer before each new attempt, instead of waking the phone every few minutes.
- Receive offers a fresh address after every sync, Live's included. It used to keep showing an address that a payment had just reached, until Gerfaut restarted.
- Picking `Automatic` as the public server works again after you picked a named one.
- The welcome tour opened from **Settings** > **About** closes with `Skip` and `Get started`.
- A restore or a broadcast that the app lock interrupts now finishes. The restored wallets show, and the transaction joins `Recent broadcasts`.
- **Policy** > **Descriptor** shows and copies the whole wallet as one multipath descriptor, with `/<0;1>/*`. It used to show the receive branch alone, which watched the wallet without its change.
- When the server itself refuses addresses, **Settings** > **Notifications** says so, instead of blaming Live's own limits.
- When a server takes only a certain number of your addresses, Live asks it again for all of them a day later, or an hour later when it took none. It used to wait until you changed the server settings or Live started again, which could leave Live off for days. Two refusals still hold until you change the server settings or Live starts again. One is a single address the server turns down, such as one with a history too long for it. The other is a cut from an ElectrumX server when Live uses too much of its resources, after which Live asks for fewer addresses.
- In Add a wallet, a QR code with no receive or change path brings a notice. Gerfaut assumes the usual `0/*` and `1/*` and asks you to compare the first address with your signing device.
- The price widget gives the date of a price that is not from today, as in `as of Oct 03, 09:41`. It used to give the time alone, so a price kept for days read like one from this morning.
- `Synced 5 min ago` and the other lines that count time since an event move on while a page stays open.
- A page that fails to load, such as a wallet, its UTXOs, a transaction or the wallet list, says why and offers `Try again`. A list of UTXOs that failed to load no longer reads as an empty one.
- A refused rename stays in its dialog with the reason. A failed `Load older transactions` says why under the button, instead of in a toast gone before you read it.
- TalkBack names every switch, field and checkbox, reads errors aloud, says `Hidden amount` instead of reading out dots, and reads a transaction id once.
- Every small target, the address chips included, is at least 44 dp high.
- The app lock sheet takes only digits for a PIN, on the number keyboard, and counts its length the way Gerfaut does when it saves it.
- Saving a file to a slow cloud provider no longer freezes the screen.
- Gerfaut no longer requires a camera to install. Scanning stays optional.
- A wallet added on another network opens even when switching to that network fails.
- On the import screen, the first address belongs to the network you pick and changes when you pick another one. A key added on regtest shows its `bcrt1` address, not the `tb1` address of signet.
- While `Test the connection` starts the built-in Tor, the Tor card counts up in steps of ten, as in `Starting the built-in Tor… 40%`. The card used to stay still until the test ended.
- Forgetting a certificate says that syncs with that server fail until you select **Save backend** again and accept it. When the server does not answer at `Save backend`, the note says the same of a server that signs its own certificate. Both texts used to say that Gerfaut would ask again at the next connection, which it does not.
- On an Esplora server, Live polls every address in turn. It used to start over at the top of the list each time it reconnected, every half hour. It never reached the addresses further down, and a payment to one of them waited for the next sync.

### Security

- The price goes through Tor whenever one of your nodes is a `.onion` address, on any network. Gerfaut asks for no price while Tor is out of reach, because the price sources would otherwise see the phone's IP address. The Tor card says so. Gerfaut also stops asking for the price while it is out of sight.
- With a `.onion` node, the price widget never starts Tor for the price alone. While Gerfaut is closed, the widget asks for the price only through a Tor that already runs, such as the one a sync started or Orbot. Otherwise it keeps the last price, with the time Gerfaut fetched it.
- While an app lock is set, the balance widget shows no wallet name and no amount. On the `Widgets` card in **Settings** > **Notifications**, Gerfaut greys out `Show balances on widgets` until you remove the lock.
- Under the calculator disguise, a vault that fails to open shows the calculator, not Gerfaut's error page.
- Gerfaut no longer leaves a transaction history shared as CSV in its cache. It deletes the copy the share sheet needed at the next share, or when it next starts.
