Get the next unused receive address of a wallet, with its QR code, and audit every address the wallet revealed. Gerfaut derives each address from the descriptor, so you never hand out the same one twice.

<Warning>
Verify the address on your signing device before you share it. Gerfaut never holds the keys behind an address, and a compromised computer or phone could show you a different one.
</Warning>

## Get an address

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Receive page">
    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Desktop">Select **Receive** in the sidebar.</Tab>
      <Tab title="Android">Select **Receive** at the bottom of the wallet page.</Tab>
    </Tabs>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the address">
    The card reads `Next unused address · index N`, the full address, and its `Derivation path`, such as `m/84'/0'/0'/0/5`. Select the QR code to enlarge it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy it">
    Select **Copy address**. The button reads `Copied` for a moment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

  <Accordion title="Read the path of a key added without its origin">
    The `Derivation path` then reads from the key itself, such as `m/0/5`.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Receive on a wallet added from a single address">
    The card reads `Watched address` and shows the one address it watches.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Skip to another address

Select **Next address** to skip ahead one unused address at a time. The label then reads `Unused address · index N`. Select **First unused** to come back.

Skipping retires nothing. An address only counts as used once the chain shows a transaction on it.

<Note>
Past the gap limit, an amber note warns that other wallet software may not detect coins received that far ahead. The gap limit is 20 by default and applies to every wallet: change it in **Settings** > **Wallets**.
</Note>

## Audit the revealed addresses

Two cards follow the receive block:

- **External:** The receive addresses, in derivation order.
- **Change:** The internal addresses used by outgoing transactions. The card reads `No change addresses revealed yet.` until one exists.

Each row shows the index, the address as a chip to copy, a `Used` or `Fresh` pill, and the balance the address holds, or a dash at zero. The card header counts the rows, and on desktop the used ones.

Each card shows five rows. Select **Show all** to unfold it and **Show less** to fold it back. Each list stops at the first 200 addresses of its keychain.
